## How the decision picker works

Type in your options, one at a time, and spin the wheel. When it stops, the option under the pointer is your pick. Behind the scenes, the tool uses your browser's random number generator to choose a result, and every option has an equal chance of being picked. The spin animation is just for fun. It doesn't change the outcome.

You can add as many options as you like, but two to eight usually works best. More than that and the slices get small and harder to read.

## What it's good for

The decision wheel is made for small, low-stakes choices where you'd be happy with any of the options:

- What to have for dinner or where to order from.
- Which movie or show to watch tonight.
- Who goes first in a game, or who picks the music in the car.
- Which chore to do first, or which workout to try.
- Picking a name from a list for a classroom activity or giveaway among friends.

It's also a neutral way to settle a group decision. Nobody can say the wheel played favorites.

## What it's not for

Please don't use a random wheel for important decisions about health, money, work, legal matters or relationships. Those deserve real thought and, often, advice from someone you trust. A good rule of thumb: if you'd be upset about one of the options, don't put it on the wheel.

## A useful trick

Even when you can't decide, the wheel can help you figure out what you actually want. Spin it and notice your reaction. If you feel a little disappointed with the result, that's a sign you were leaning toward a different option. Our guide on [how to use a random picker to make decisions faster](/articles/how-to-use-a-random-picker-to-decide-faster) explains this and a few other tips.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the wheel truly random?

It uses your browser's built-in random number function, which is more than random enough for everyday choices. Read [how random number generators work](/articles/how-random-number-generators-work) for more.

### Why did it pick the same thing twice in a row?

Streaks are a normal part of randomness. Each spin is independent, so repeats happen.

### Are my options saved?

Your options stay on your device and aren't sent to us.
