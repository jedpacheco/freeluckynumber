Ask a room full of people to name a lucky number and you'll hear "seven" more than anything else. It's on slot machines, in fairy tales and in more song titles than anyone could count. When the British math writer Alex Bellos ran a large online survey in 2014 asking people to name their favorite number, more than 30,000 people answered, and 7 came out on top by a clear margin.

So why 7? There isn't one neat answer. It's more like a pile of reasons that have been building up for thousands of years, from the sky, from religion, from how our brains handle information and from plain old repetition. Let's go through the main ones.

## Seven things in the sky

Long before telescopes, people looking up at night could see seven objects that moved across the sky differently from the stars: the Sun, the Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. Ancient astronomers in Mesopotamia paid close attention to them, and they became important in religion and timekeeping.

That's a big reason we have a seven-day week. Babylonian astronomers tracked the Moon's phases, which run about seven days each, and the planets became linked with days. You can still see it in English and other European languages. Saturday is Saturn's day, Sunday the Sun's and Monday the Moon's. In Spanish, martes, miércoles, jueves and viernes come from Mars, Mercury, Jupiter and Venus. So every week of your life is quietly built around the number seven.

## Seven in religion and stories

Seven shows up all over religious texts. In the Bible, the world is created in six days and the seventh is a day of rest. There are seven deadly sins, seven virtues and seven seals in the book of Revelation. In Islam, pilgrims on the Hajj walk around the Kaaba seven times, and there are said to be seven heavens. Hinduism describes seven chakras in popular modern teachings and seven steps in a traditional wedding ceremony called saptapadi.

Folklore loves it too. There are seven dwarfs, seven seas, seven wonders of the ancient world and seven-league boots. Once a number becomes a storytelling habit, each new story makes it feel even more special.

## Why our brains might like 7

There's also a psychological angle. In 1956, the psychologist George Miller published a famous paper called "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two." He argued that people can hold about seven items in short-term memory at once. Later research suggests the real figure is closer to four chunks of information, but the paper made "seven" part of pop psychology.

Another reason is that 7 feels random. When people are asked to pick a number between 1 and 10, they pick 7 far more often than any other. Researchers think it's because 7 doesn't feel "too obvious." It's not even like 2 or 10, not in the middle like 5 and not at the edge like 1. It's prime, so it doesn't divide neatly into anything. That gives it a slightly odd, individual feel, and people seem to like that.

## Lucky 7 in games

Gambling helped make 7 the face of luck in the modern world. Slot machines started using the triple 7 as a top jackpot symbol in the early 1900s, and it stuck.

Dice are part of the story too, but in an interesting way. When you roll two six-sided dice, 7 is the most common total. There are six ways to make it (1 and 6, 2 and 5, 3 and 4, and each of those flipped), out of 36 possible rolls. That gives it a 1 in 6 chance, higher than any other sum. In the game of craps, rolling a 7 on your first throw is a win. So 7 really does come up more often with two dice, which is about math, not magic.

## Seven around the world

Seven isn't lucky everywhere, which is part of what makes number superstitions fun. In Japan, 7 is generally considered lucky, and there's a group of folk deities called the Seven Lucky Gods. In China, 7 is more mixed. The seventh month of the lunar calendar is known as Ghost Month, when some people believe spirits roam, so it's seen as a bad time to move house or get married. Meanwhile 8 is the big lucky number there. We cover that in more detail in [lucky and unlucky numbers around the world](/articles/lucky-and-unlucky-numbers-around-the-world).

## Is 7 actually lucky?

In the sense of making good things happen, no. A 7 on a lottery ticket has exactly the same chance of being drawn as any other number. A coin doesn't know what day of the week it is. And a house number 7 won't bring better fortune than number 12.

But luck isn't only about outcomes. Having a favorite number can be part of who you are. Athletes pick jersey number 7 because of players they grew up watching. Couples get married on July 7. People set their alarms for 7:07. It's a small ritual that makes a day feel a bit more personal, and that's a perfectly good reason to like it.

What we'd suggest avoiding is the idea that picking 7 gives you an edge in a game of chance. It doesn't, and if lots of other people pick it too, you'd actually have to share any prize with more of them. ## Seven in numerology

In numerology, 7 is seen as the number of the thinker and the seeker. People with a 7 [life path number](/articles/what-your-life-path-number-says-about-you) are described as curious, private and drawn to big questions. As with all numerology, that's a tradition, not a scientific finding, but it fits the mysterious reputation 7 has picked up over the centuries.
